What this report is

An analytics report, not a hot-take page

We take a team's numbers — the hereditary numbers, what they actually did, season by season — and we look for the pattern behind staying great. Public data only. AI does the reading; a human checks it.

How to read it

Pick who you are below

Stat head, manager, fan, regular Joe — the report meets you where you are. Numbers are regular-season wins unless it says otherwise. A percentage is a percentage of provenness: how much the numbers back the read. Nothing here is a prediction.

If a line is wrong, the fact beats the line. Say so and it changes.

The intent

Find the pattern, translate it

Find what the great teams did to sustain it, then translate that pattern one-for-one to where a team is at — its market, its resources, its restrictions. Show the picture clearer than the emotion side alone. The other sides of the game make the picture finer later. The framing never moves.

"People use analytics wrong. They either use analytics all the way or they use emotions all the way. There is a balance. You use the numbers to prove if the emotions are right — it just gives you a percentage, a percentage of provenness. Once you see it from that lens, you know when or when not to use analytics."
— The rule this report runs on. Analytics doesn't replace the gut. It scores it.

Managers — what to do with it

You run a team. The report's job is to show you what the sustainers did that you can do with what you have — inside your sport's rules, inside your market, inside your restrictions.

The pattern gets translated one-for-one to where your team is at. A small-market team will never sustain greatness like the Patriots or Kansas City, move for move. But Kansas City had to do something to get in position to sustain greatness in the first place. That something is what this report hunts for.

What you get: the fix options within your sport's rules, the pattern translated to your resources, and an honest read on your place in the league — because knowing your place is the starting point, not the finish line.

Team reports are built to order — that is the client intent. No team client yet; this page is the frame. The pattern work lands here first.

Regular Fans — the story straight

Kansas City. They were garbage — 2–14 in 2012. Then they went up — and they stayed up. Twelve straight winning seasons, three Super Bowls, same coach the whole way. The carrot is what they did back then, not the team today. The run ended in 2025 at 6–11. The question is what they did to get in position.

New England. 5–11 in 2000. Super Bowl the next year. Seven straight seasons of 11 wins or more, three Super Bowls. Then the fall — down to 4–13, twice. Then 2025: 14–3 and a Super Bowl trip under a new coach. The question is what broke, and what came back.

Philadelphia. Up, down, up, down, up. Super Bowl in 2017. 4–11–1 in 2020. Super Bowl trip in 2022. Super Bowl in 2024. And 3–7 right now, in 2026. The question is what keeps pulling them back up — and what keeps knocking them down. The collapse is the stuff the numbers alone can't see. That's the drama side, and it comes later.

Regular Joes — what it means

Some teams get good and stay good. Most don't. This report figures out what the stayers did different — in plain numbers, straight off public data — so any team can steal the parts that fit where they live. The numbers don't decide anything. They just tell you how much your gut is backed up. That's it. That's the whole report.

Sustain greatness

NFL first — the proof sport. Two tracks: the Pats and KC, both traced back from a shitty start to great. Find the pattern. Two different patterns is a good outcome, not a failure.

The sustainer — the carrot is what they did

Kansas City

12 straight winning seasons · 3 Super Bowls

From garbage to sustained. 2012: 2–14 — the bottom. 2013–2024, never a losing season — 11 wins or more in ten of those twelve years. Same coach the whole way. The carrot is what they DID — the build, not the current team. The run ended in 2025 at 6–11. That's the record, not the model.

The question: what did they do to get in position to sustain greatness?

The fall and the bounce-back

New England

7 straight 11+ win seasons · 3 Super Bowls

2000: 5–11. 2001: Super Bowl. Then the machine — 2003, 2004, two more Super Bowls, 16–0 in 2007. Seven straight seasons of 11 wins or more from 2013, three more Super Bowls. Then the fall: 7–9, 8–9, 4–13, 4–13. Then 2025: 14–3 and a Super Bowl trip under a new coach.

The question: what broke — and what came back?

The burst

Philadelphia

Super Bowl 2017 · 4–11–1 in 2020 · Super Bowl 2024

Up, down, up, down, up. A Super Bowl, then a 4–11–1 season three years later. A Super Bowl trip, then a Super Bowl. And right now: 3–7 in 2026. The collapse case isn't history — it's live. The Eagles are the control case for the whole report: what keeps pulling a burst team back up — and what keeps knocking it down?

The question: what separates a burst from a sustain? The collapse is the drama side, and the numbers alone can't see it — yet.

The pattern

What we're looking for. The report doesn't fully answer these yet — it shows the picture clearer than the emotion side alone, then the other aspects make the picture finer.

  1. Is there a pattern between what Kansas City did, what the Patriots did, and the other teams that sustain greatness?
  2. What separates the sustainers from the bursts — the teams that go up and come back down?
  3. How does the pattern translate one-for-one to a team's market, its resources, and its restrictions — what can it do with what it has?
