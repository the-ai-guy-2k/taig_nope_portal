The method, in order

Take a bad team, go back to the hereditary numbers — what the great teams were before they were great — find the pattern in what they did, then run the what-if: if the bad team did that, inside the rules of their sport, where do they land.

  1. Hereditary numbers. Start with what the team carried into the season — the record, the affiliations, the park, the recent failures. The 2021–2025 table on the Numbers page is the baseline.
  2. The pattern. Find what the great teams did to sustain it, then translate that pattern one-for-one to where a team is at — its market, its resources, its restrictions. Show the picture clearer than the emotion side alone.
  3. The what-if. State the tests timestamped, in terms that can be scored without moving the goalposts. Five tests, filed Nov 25, 2026, on the What-Ifs page.
  4. Publish first. Out in the open, timestamped, so anybody can prove it wrong. That is what makes it real instead of a pitch.

Data — public for now

On-field data is gettable from public markets. Inside-club data is not — only if the club shares it.

What is public

On-field data

Pitch-level Statcast for all of Triple-A since 2023 via Baseball Savant. Team and player totals free via the MLB Stats API, MiLB.com, Baseball-Reference, and FanGraphs.

What is not

Inside-club data

Development plans, medical, scouting grades, internal video, proprietary models — only if the club shares them. This report uses none of that, because it does not have it.

The legal catch

Research use, not commercial

The free feeds are free for personal and research use, not commercial use. The paid product needs a licensed feed (Sportradar / Stats Perform, quote-only) or club-shared data. This report is the proof, published in public — not the paid product.

Sources and limits

Public data only. No club, league, or player information beyond what is published for anyone to read was used.

Sources: 2025 International League and Jacksonville team totals — Baseball-Reference Register; year-by-year franchise records and run totals — The Baseball Cube; roster and individual season totals cross-check — StatsCrew; championship and history reporting — MiLB.com (International League championship, Triple-A National Championship, Jax history timeline); 2026 record — MiLB, Sportradar, Baseball-Reference, The Baseball Cube.

Limits, stated plainly:

— Triple-A totals reflect constant roster movement, including major-league rehab assignments and call-ups, so individual totals are partial-season by design.

— Jacob Berry's games differ by one between registers: Baseball-Reference 124, The Baseball Cube / StatsCrew 123. Batting totals are identical.

— The full-season close-game splits (one-run 29–14, ≤3-run 57–33) rest on the club's September 2025 game notes. Sourced, not independently re-derived — confirm before publication.

— The Pythagorean check uses the standard runs-scored and runs-allowed formula and is presented as an expectation to test against, not as a verdict on any single game.

— The 2026 season is complete at 73–74 (42–32 first half, 31–42 second; season finale rained out). The five what-ifs are not scored on this report — scoring them is Tee's call.

— The comp-club pick is open. The two obvious graphs are surfaced by the client intake, not named on this report. No prices appear on this report — product first, pricing follows.
