How they actually won.

Prevention. Closeness. Depth over a single star.

Put plainly: pitching and defense kept every game within reach, and Jacksonville took more of those reachable games than anyone else in their league.

They won the close ones

For this team, close-game execution was the offense's missing power — it turned a +86 run differential into a first-place finish, a first-half title, and home field for the league final. Close-game splits below are sourced from the club's September 2025 game notes — see the Method page.

One-run games

29–14 .674

Tied for the most one-run wins in the International League.

Decided by 3 or fewer

57–33 .633

64.8 percent of Jacksonville's wins to that point in the season had come by three runs or fewer.

Run differential

+86 693–607

A standard Pythagorean check on 693 scored and 607 allowed expects about .565 — roughly an 85–65 team. Jacksonville finished 89–61, about four wins better than that expectation, and the gap lives in those one-run and three-run games.

Depth over a single star

No Jacksonville regular carried this team the way a Triple-A MVP carries a team. That is a strength of the read, not a weakness in it — five different ways of producing a run, spread across five different players, none of whom had to be great every night for the total to work.

Most complete regular

Jacob Berry

.261/.348/.394 in 124 games* — the best batting average among Jacksonville regulars — with a team-leading 28 doubles, 54 runs batted in (tied for the team lead), and 27 stolen bases.

*Games differ by one between registers: Baseball-Reference 124, The Baseball Cube / StatsCrew 123. Batting totals are identical.

The power

Joe Mack

Team-leading 18 home runs while hitting .250/.320/.459 in 100 games, with 53 runs batted in.

Run production

Deyvison De Los Santos

Matched Berry's 54 runs batted in, with 12 home runs.

On-base and speed

Jakob Marsee

A .379 on-base percentage, 68 walks, 14 home runs, and a team-leading 47 stolen bases in 98 games.

Volume

Maximo Acosta

116 games, 13 home runs, 33 stolen bases. As a team, Jacksonville stole 305 bases. 42 different players batted for Jacksonville in 2025 — the title did not rest on a lineup that stayed intact all year, because at Triple-A no lineup does.

The pitching

Roa · Snelling · Mazur

Christian Roa went 10–2 with a 2.83 ERA in 50 relief appearances, striking out 64 in 60⅓ innings — the kind of bullpen arm a 29–14 one-run record is built on. Robby Snelling was dominant in 11 starts: 6–2, 1.27 ERA, 81 strikeouts in 63⅔ innings. Adam Mazur carried the heaviest starting load, 107⅓ innings with 100 strikeouts. Janson Junk added a 2.68 ERA across 10 appearances. Team notes also credited Jacksonville with leading Triple-A in defensive efficiency for much of the season.

The championship weeks were the season in miniature

A team that only wins one way loses one of those two games. Jacksonville won both.

Sept 25 — IL final, Game 3

7–4 over Scranton/WB

At VyStar Ballpark. Jacksonville scored five runs in the first inning of the deciding game and closed the series 2–1. Early pressure, then enough pitching to make it stand.

Sept 27 — National final

8–7 over Las Vegas

At Las Vegas Ballpark. Jacksonville led, Las Vegas scored five in the top of the ninth to take a 7–6 lead, and the season came down to the bottom of the ninth in the other team's park. Jack Winkler singled, and with one out Jacob Berry hit a two-run walk-off home run, measured at 429 feet, to win 8–7. Berry was named the game's Most Valuable Player.

Percentage of provenness

How much of the title was already proven over 150 games — and the one slice that still has to be earned.

Proven: both halves. First half 47–28 (.627), second half 42–33 (.560) — a winning half after the playoff berth was secured, in a league where rosters turn over constantly with call-ups and rehab assignments.

Proven: home and road. 44–30 at home, 45–31 on the road. Park and crowd were part of the season; they were not the reason for it.

Proven: run prevention over volume. 607 runs allowed across 150 games, second-fewest per game in Triple-A late in the season, with 42 players batting. Not a two-week sample.

Not yet proven: the close-game edge at this size. Some of the one-run surplus is real skill — bullpen quality, defense, late-inning execution. Some of it, in any single season, is sequencing that does not repeat at the same rate. The foundation was proven. The size of the close-game surplus is the part 2026 has to prove again.

The read in one line: the championship was mostly built on repeatable things, and the one repeat that still has to be earned is the biggest one — turning close games into wins at a .674 clip in one-run games, year after year.
