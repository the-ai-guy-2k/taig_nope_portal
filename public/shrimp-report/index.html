The read, in one page

Three facts carry the 2025 season. Everything else on this site is the proof behind them.

2025 — the season

89–61 .593

Champions, twice

First in the International League East. Beat Scranton/Wilkes-Barre 2–1 for the club's first International League title since 1968 — then beat Las Vegas 8–7 for the Triple-A National Championship. Most wins for a Jacksonville club since 1964.

How they won

607 allowed

Prevention, not power

693 scored, 607 allowed — a +86 differential. The offense ranked in the bottom third of Triple-A. The pitching and defense kept every game within reach, and the Shrimp took more of those reachable games than anyone else in their league.

2026 — the season happened

73–74 .497

The burst, then the fall

42–32 in the first half, 31–42 in the second. 738 scored, 730 allowed — still a positive differential, +8. The 2026 what-if scoring waits on Tee's call — the record is stated here, the verdicts are not scored yet.

Sustain greatness means the pipeline

For a Triple-A club, the record is not the measure. Development is.

Getting great is one problem. Staying great is a different one, and it is the only one worth tracking from here. At Triple-A, rosters are rebuilt by the parent club every winter — holding any team to the same win total with a different roster would be a dishonest test.

So the test is the pipeline: development means experiencing close to the real thing before doing the real things. The club's goal is quality players into the major league — not necessarily the parent club, any majors club counts. The measure is majors arrivals, not wins. The trackers in the Shrimp's lane are records, playoffs, and chips.
